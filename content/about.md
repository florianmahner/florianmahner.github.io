I am a postdoctoral researcher in machine learning at TU Berlin and the [Berlin Institute for the Foundations of Learning and Data](https://www.bifold.berlin/), working with [Klaus-Robert Müller](https://web.ml.tu-berlin.de/author/prof.-dr.-klaus-robert-muller/). I did my PhD at the [Max Planck Research Group Vision and Computational Cognition](https://www.cbs.mpg.de/independent-research-groups/vision-and-computational-cognition) supervised by [Martin Hebart](https://martin-hebart.de/).

My research focuses on representation learning in humans and machines. I combine insights from cognitive science and machine learning to understand the similarities and differences between human and artificial systems. I primarily work on vision, language, and multimodal learning.

A central theme of my work is representational alignment and universal representations, with a focus on interpretability. In recent work, I have [tested machine learning models like human participants](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-025-01041-7) in behavioral experiments, and studied [what different models have in common](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13675). To this end, I have developed [statistical learning methods](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26921) to uncover interpretable structure in brains, behavior, and machine representations.
